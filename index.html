About

I am a fifth-year graduate student in Astrophysical Sciences at Princeton University, advised by Anatoly Spitkovsky. I study how plasma processes on microscopic scales power the emission we observe from extreme astrophysical systems, particularly neutron star magnetospheres, using a combination of kinetic plasma simulations and analytic modeling. A central focus of my work has been studying the interacting magnetospheres of pre-merger binary neutron stars, aiming to predict electromagnetic precursor signals of relevance to upcoming multimessenger observing programs, using global kinetic simulations. I am also a member of the Simons Collaboration on the Extreme Electrodynamics of Compact Sources (SCEECS).

I am originally from Montréal, Québec, and completed my undergraduate degree in Honours Physics at McGill University, where I worked on cosmological line-intensity mapping.
